# Column: Effective Cost

## Side-by-Side Conceptual Comparison: Billed vs. Effective Cost

This table provides a structured, side-by-side breakdown of the definitions of Billed Cost and Effective Cost. The goal is to ensure that both definitions address all relevant aspects and use cases, highlight similarities and differences, and use consistent terminology wherever possible.

For instance, FOCUS 1.2 version of the Effective Cost definition does not clearly explain what the concept represents. Instead, it focused primarily on the mechanics of calculation (such as amortization, proportional attribution, and exclusions)without first establishing its conceptual role within the cost model.

Items flagged in the Comment column represent open discussion points:

- TBD-1: Treatment of Billed Cost in No-Upfront and Partial scenarios
- TBD-2: Whether the Effective Cost definition for Marketplace offerings should explicitly address cases where the purchase and the associated eligible usage originate from different DataGenerators.

If the charge is covered, its Billed Cost is zero, as the attributed portions are invoiced through the original one-time or recurring purchases that provide the coverage. Billed Cost is commonly used in FinOps activities that rely on cash-basis accounting, such as cost allocation, budgeting, invoice reconciliation, and reporting of actual invoiced amounts. | Effective Cost represents the accrual-based cost associated with a charge (cost recognized when incurred rather than on a cash-basis), inclusive of all applicable reduced unit prices and discounts, while excluding any portions of cost designated to cover associated eligible charges. Each charge is treated as a whole, with all applicable discounts applied uniformly to the entire charge. It may be independent, covered by other charges, designated to cover other eligible charges, or both. In all cases, these relationships apply to the charge in its entirety. For independent charges, the Effective Cost equals the Billed Cost. If the charge is covered, its Effective Cost reflects the sum of portions of cost invoiced as part of related one-time or recurring purchases (e.g., commitment purchases or marketplace offerings purchases) and attributed to cover this charge. Attributed portions should be proportional to the charge's Pricing Quantity. The Effective Cost may draw from multiple related purchase records, but must not "blend" costs from mutually unrelated purchases (e.g., purchases for different commitments or marketplace offerings). In such instances, the Billed Cost is zero. If the charge is designated to cover associated eligible charges, its Effective Cost is zero, as its cost is distributed across those charges. This cost is denominated in the Billing Currency. Effective Cost is commonly used to track and analyze spending trends from a usage perspective and for accrual-based reporting, providing insight into the cost of resources or services regardless of invoice timing. |

## Example Scenarios

### 7 Day Full upfront Reservation

#### On the day of purchase a row exists for the upfront cost of the reservation as follows

| Charge period           | Billing period           | Charge Category | Units | Billed Cost | Effective Cost |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------- | ----- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Jan 1 2023 - Jan 1 2024 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | Purchase    | 3     | 26.69589041 | 0              |

*Note: Billed cost is showing the total cash outlay for the reservation. Also note there is no initial Reserved Instance fee representing the cost of the RI for the applicable period.*

___

#### Also on each hour a row is added as follows

| Charge period           | Billing period           | Charge Category | Units | Billed Cost | Effective Cost |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------- | ----- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Jan 1 2023 - Jan 2 2023 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | Usage       | 3     | 0           | 3.81369863     |

*Note: The amortization is equal to the (Upfront fee / (number of normalized units reserved x periods) x normalized units consumed).*

___

#### If the Reserved Instance is unused during a period, for example another ineligible instance type is used

| Charge period           | Billing period           | Charge Category     | Units | Billed Cost | Effective Cost |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------- | ----- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Jan 4 2023 - Jan 5 2023 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | OD Usag         | 3     | 5.544       | 5.544          |
| Jan 4 2023 - Jan 5 2023 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | Unused RI Usage | 3     | 0           | 3.81369863     |

*Note: For the On Demand usage the Billed cost and Effective cost are the same with an Amortization of Zero. Since the RI went completely unused during the period there is an unused RI usage row with the an amortization equal to (Upfront fee / (number of normalized units reserved x periods) x normalized units.*

___

#### If the RI is partially unused during a period, for example a scale down occurs

| Charge period           | Billing period           | Charge Category     | Units | Billed Cost | Effective Cost |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------- | ----- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Jan 4 2023 - Jan 5 2023 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | OD Usage        | 1     | 0           | 2.542465753          |
| Jan 4 2023 - Jan 5 2023 | Jan 1 2023 - Jan 31 2023 | Unused RI Usage | 2     | 0           | 1.271232877     |

*Note: For the Reserved Instance Usage the Billed cost is zero as this RI is full upfront. It is especially important to note that the usage units must be normalized for this formula to be applicable in cases of commitment discount flexibility (e.g. c7g.large normalized units is 4, c7g.medium is 2). Effective cost is the same as amortization because the upfront fee covers all of the compute usage expense for this row.*
