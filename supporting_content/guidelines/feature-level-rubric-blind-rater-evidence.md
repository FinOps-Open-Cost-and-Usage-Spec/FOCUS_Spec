# Feature-Level Rubric: Blind-Rater Evidence

This document records the blind-rater testing behind the [feature-level rubric guideline](../../guidelines/contributors/feature-level-rubric-guidelines.md) (FR 2335). It is development evidence, not specification content. Companion document: [design notes](feature-level-rubric-design-notes.md), which record the thought process and method behind the guideline itself.

**What the levels in this document are.** Every level shown here is a rater's output produced under the draft guideline with published levels removed. They measure whether the guideline text determines an answer; they are not proposed levels for any column. Level changes to existing columns are adjudicated separately, through the working group's review of the guideline and of the open level-change pull requests.

## What This Evidence Covers

Rounds 1 through 8 tested a fuller draft than the current guideline. That draft covered all four feature levels and the prior question of whether a proposed column belongs in the schema; the current guideline is scoped to the Mandatory and Conditional criteria and defers the rest. The rounds are retained in full, because the passages they exercised most heavily are the ones the narrowed guideline keeps, but the mapping needs stating precisely:

* **The rounds do carry over as evidence for the surviving text.** The applicability and producibility tests, the presence-versus-nullability discriminator, the earned-Mandatory bar, the interim boundary rule, and the directional derivation principle were unchanged from the text rounds 7 and 8 exercised through round 10, and the restructure has since moved them as the Revision Boundary below records. The sentinels that converged unanimously in round 8, the last round against that text, converged on those passages: [BilledCost](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/billedcost.md), [EffectiveCost](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/effectivecost.md), [ChargeClass](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/chargeclass.md), [ContractCommitmentId](../../specification/datasets/contract_commitment/columns/contractcommitmentid.md), [RegionId](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/regionid.md), [AvailabilityZone](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/availabilityzone.md), [InvoiceId](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/invoiceid.md), [ListUnitPrice](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/listunitprice.md), and [ContractedUnitPrice](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/contractedunitprice.md).
* **The sentinels that never converged turned mainly on deferred material.** [BillingAccountName](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/billingaccountname.md)'s split ran through the necessity refinement carve-out in every round it appeared, and the round-8 outcomes for [ListCost](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/listcost.md) and [ContractedCost](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/contractedcost.md) were a held-out-of-schema reading against a level the fuller draft forbade. All three of those routes are admission decisions the current guideline no longer makes, so those columns are now leveled by applicability alone.
* **One unresolved observation is in scope and stands.** Raters repeatedly acknowledged a boundary case and then chose Mandatory over the interim rule's Conditional default, first in round 6 and again in round 7. That default now sits in Tie-Breakers and Defaults, and whether it needs reinforcing is a live question.
* **The narrowed text was tested separately, in round 9.** Rounds 1 through 8 should be read as evidence about the surviving passages rather than as validation of this revision. Round 9 tested the narrowed text directly, under a reduced packet, and is the round to read for this revision's own determinacy. One round-8 observation is now moot: the ambiguity flagged in AvailabilityZone's Condition name came from an inheritance sentence that no longer exists, so that column is decided directly by applicability.

## Revision Boundary

Every round recorded below ran against the guideline as it was structured before the restructure on this branch, and the principle numbers, input numbers, and section names each round uses are the ones in force at the time of that round. They do not resolve against the current text. From round 5 onward that text stated seven numbered principles, two decision inputs, and an Interim Boundary Rule; rounds 1 through 4 predate that structure. The current guideline states five principles and a five-step Decision Procedure, with the rest moved into Applicability Signals and Tie-Breakers and Defaults.

The numbering also shifted once between round 9 and round 10, when the two fabrication principles merged and the Supported Features write-back was added, so the same number means different things in different rounds:

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Principle 3 | Principle 3 | Principle 3, the two axes |
| Principle 4 | Principle 4 | Step 2's applicability test and `Conditional` default, and Tie-Breakers and Defaults |
| Principles 5 and 6 | Principle 5 | Principle 4, fabrication |
| Principle 7 | Principle 6 | Principle 5, derivation |
| Did not exist | Principle 7 | Not carried forward |
| Input 1 and input 2 | Input 1 and input 2 | The two halves of Step 2, the Applicability Test |
| Input 1's sub-bullets | Input 1's sub-bullets | The Applicability Signals table |
| The Interim Boundary Rule | The Interim Boundary Rule | Tie-Breakers and Defaults |

Principles 1 and 2 are not reconstructable from this record, and neither is the necessity refinement carve-out that drove BillingAccountName in rounds 5 through 8. Where a round cites one of those, read it as text the current guideline does not carry forward under the same name.

The boundary covers more than numbering. Where a round describes the guideline's worked examples, or names the columns its text mentions, those describe the pre-restructure text as well. The current guideline carries one Worked Example, RegionId.

**One tested item is not carried forward.** The Supported Features write-back, added as principle 7 by the passes that followed round 9 and tested in round 10, is not a principle in the current guideline. What stands in its place is narrower: Step 2 states a Supported Feature floor that bars `Recommended` and `Optional` for a Directly Dependent Column, and what a Supported Feature does about a column that can be absent is listed as a Deferred Topic belonging to the Supported Features work. Round 10's observations about principle 7 are therefore findings about text the guideline no longer contains, and are retained as history rather than as current state.

**The variance rule has since changed shape.** Round 10 tested a sub-bullet that had two branches, one of which it found unstated, and recorded the completion of that sentence. The guideline no longer has two branches to state: a gate on variance never lowers the level, and the signal's `Mandatory` verdict applies where the gated concept is itself universal. Round 10's findings about the admissible branch, and the [PricingCurrency](../../specification/datasets/cost_and_usage/columns/pricingcurrency.md) split that exposed it, describe a rule the guideline no longer contains.

**No round has run against the restructured text.** Rounds 1 through 10 are evidence about the passages that survive, not validation of the current structure.

## Why Blind Raters

The guideline states its own bar: two people applying the principles to the same column should reach the same level, without the author in the room. Blind rating tests that bar directly and cheaply before human inter-rater testing. Raters receive the guideline and the evidence a leveling decision needs, with every column's published feature level, nullability, and normative requirements removed, and are instructed not to recall published levels. Where the text under-determines an answer, isolated raters diverge; where it determines one, they converge. Divergence and ungroundable answers are the signal, not agreement, because published levels appear in model training data and agreement alone could reflect memory.

## Method

Each rater receives a single packet file and may read nothing else:

* **Part A.** The guideline, verbatim, at the revision under test.
* **Part B.** The FOCUS Supported Features content, as evidence for the necessity input.
* **Part C.** A roster of other Cost and Usage columns and their current levels, for the derivability input, with all columns under test removed.
* **Part D.** The columns to classify, each as a redacted evidence pack: overview, description, and content constraints kept; feature level, nullability, and normative requirements removed.

Parts B and C served the necessity and derivability inputs, which the narrowed guideline no longer has. Round 9 ran on a reduced packet without them.

Protocol rules: every classification includes verbatim quotes of the Part A sentences that governed each decision input, so memorized answers are separable from grounded ones; a rater who finds no governing sentence records an empty quote and describes the missing sentence; raters flag any column whose level could flip on the boundary threshold the guideline defers to the companion mechanics guideline. BilledCost serves as a canary in every round: a rater or a revision that moves it off Mandatory fails the round.

**The regression the arms were built to find.** In the treatment arm one column moved against its control: InvoiceId fell from Conditional in two of three raters to one of three. The cause was traced to two sentences the plain-language pass had weakened. Principle 4's "The default between them is Conditional" had become "When it is genuinely unclear which of the two applies, the answer is Conditional," and the Interim Boundary Rule's list heading "The rule:" had become "How to choose between them:". Both were restored, and the verification arm reproduced the control arm exactly, with both recovering raters quoting the restored sentences as governing. The mechanism is worth recording because it is not simply that softer wording is weaker: the added qualifier hands the reader discretion over whether the default applies at all, so the default fires inversely with reader confidence. The rater that was uncertain applied it and reached Conditional; the rater that was confident in its own analysis judged the case not genuinely unclear, skipped the default, and reached Mandatory. A default that only binds readers who already doubt themselves does not bind the reader it most needs to.

**Two observations on unchanged text.** The weakest of the three raters returned Mandatory for InvoiceId in all three arms, and in the verification arm recorded an empty governing quote with an explicit note that no sentence establishes invoicing as universal. That reading is independent of both changes under test and stands as the open question of whether a non-invoicing operating model is an exception or a pattern. BillingAccountName remained unstable across all three arms, splitting two to one in each, with individual raters swapping sides between the control and treatment arms rather than moving together. Its instability is therefore a property of the column against this text, not an effect of either change, and it independently reproduces the display-name question the design notes record as deferred. These are rater outputs under redaction, not proposed levels.

**Edits after this round.** The guideline was edited in five passes after the verification arm ran, so the current text is not byte-identical to what round 9 tested. That is reasoning rather than evidence. The last of those is judgment-shaped, the class of wording this round showed can fire inversely with rater confidence, so it warrants a dedicated sentinel rather than riding on the existing set.

**How much of this round the guideline actually decided.** Six of the ten sentinels have their level stated or all but stated in the guideline's own worked material: BilledCost ("BilledCost clears the bar"), RegionId and ContractCommitmentId (the two worked examples, both axes), ChargeClass (principle 3's parenthetical, both axes), BillingAccountType (the variance sub-bullet's parenthetical, read with principle 5), and now ContractedCost, whose level is named in principle 7's own illustration. Unanimity on those six measures whether a rater can read the guideline, not whether the guideline determines an answer. Three more converged on Part C rather than on the guideline: for InvoiceId every rater reasoned from the roster's Invoice Detail ID, for ListCost from List Unit Price, and for BillingAccountName from Billing Account ID, with two raters stating outright that no sentence in the guideline addresses invoice-identifier or display-name columns. That leaves PricingCurrency as the only column in the round decided by the guideline's reasoning without a named answer or a roster anchor, and it split two to one.

**The variance sub-bullet states one of its two branches.** The sub-bullet says "Mandatory" twice and "Conditional" never. It writes out the level consequence when a variance gate is inadmissible, "a constant is still a truthful value and the column stays Mandatory," and states no level consequence when the gate is admissible. The reader must supply the step from "the gate is admissible" to "the column is Conditional" unaided, against a preceding sentence that says "applicability alone returns Mandatory." That asymmetry is what the two new sentinels separate. BillingAccountType, the inadmissible branch, was unanimous. PricingCurrency, the admissible branch, split: two raters inferred the missing step and reached Conditional, both flagging the gap explicitly and both lowering their confidence because of it, while the third never applied the sub-bullet to the level at all, reaching Mandatory through principle 4's universality test on the ground that every operating model prices in some currency. The strongest rater named this unprompted as the largest defect it found in the guideline, and the second reported the same gap independently in the same terms. One sentence stating what an admissible gate does to the level would close it.

**The failure mode round 9 identified was looked for and did not appear.** Round 9 found wording that fired inversely with rater confidence, binding raters who doubted themselves and releasing raters who did not. That pattern is absent here: all three raters reported Moderate confidence on PricingCurrency and still split two to one, so stated confidence did not predict the outcome. The defect this round found is a different shape, an unstated conclusion rather than a discretionary qualifier, and it separates raters by whether they noticed an omission rather than by how sure they felt. The third rater did register the variance sub-bullet, but attached it to nullability instead of to the level, which is a misrouting rather than a failure to read.

**Three observations on the new and unchanged text.** Principle 7 carried no leveling weight: no outcome turned on it, one rater cited it only to flag its recording language as duplicating the Interim Boundary Rule's, and the strongest rater used its parenthetical as applicability evidence for ContractedCost, which is circular, since that parenthetical presupposes the Conditional level rather than establishing it. Principle 7 also introduced a new named level into the guideline for a column that is also a sentinel, so ContractedCost's unanimity this round is weaker evidence than round 9's was. Separately, one rater reported that the exception path in the Interim Boundary Rule is unreachable as written, because "A single unusual operating model that lacks the column may be an exception that keeps the column Mandatory" routes any close call to a boundary the next sentence sends to Conditional, and the same rater reported that the flowchart has no branch for the variance gate, the derivation rule, or the Interim Boundary Rule's default, so a rater working from the diagram would answer PricingCurrency, ListCost, and BillingAccountType differently than one working from the prose.

**What Part C did, and what it cost.** Part C was restored to serve the variance sub-bullet, and exactly one rater used it for that purpose on exactly one column, reading Billing Currency's Mandatory level as the evidence that PricingCurrency's value is determined by another column. Everywhere else it acted as a level anchor for columns the guideline does not govern, and it moved outcomes. InvoiceId, a two to one split in round 9, was unanimous here with all three raters citing the roster. BillingAccountName, which round 9 recorded as genuinely unstable with raters swapping sides between arms, was unanimous on level here, and in the opposite direction from round 9's majority, with all three citing Billing Account ID. Neither convergence is evidence that the guideline improved. Both are evidence that a sibling roster substitutes for guidance the guideline does not supply, and the open question on display-name columns that the design notes record as deferred is untouched by it.

**Edits after this round.** Two of this round's findings were acted on, so the current text is not byte-identical to what this round tested. The variance sub-bullet was completed to name `Conditional` for the admissible branch, closing the one gap this round showed can move an outcome; the guideline has since dropped the admissible branch altogether, so a gate on variance never lowers the level and the step PricingCurrency split over no longer exists. The exception path a rater reported as unreachable was rewritten, and now sets a purported counterexample aside only by challenging whether it is admissible or whether the concept is present after all, without overriding the applicability test. The guideline was restructured after this round, so the Revision Boundary above governs how both rules read now. Confirming that the current text holds PricingCurrency together is work for a later round, and it is the column to watch.

**Two method notes.** The packet as first built did not remove the columns under test from Part C, so all nine Cost and Usage sentinels appeared in the roster with their published level and nullability, including the two new ones. The cause was a slug-matching defect in the build script, and the script's own leak check inspected only Part D, so it reported clean. The packet was rebuilt with the exclusion working and the check extended to Part C before any rater ran; the tables above are from the corrected packet. Separately, the second rater's first run terminated on an output-token limit without producing ratings, and was rerun once with a length constraint added to the prompt and the rating task otherwise unchanged.

## Caveats

* **Shared model ancestry.** Round 1 used raters sharing one base model, so unanimity there is weak evidence; later rounds mixed models. The strong signals throughout are divergence, intra-model flip-flops across identical runs, and answers a rater could not ground in a quoted sentence.
* **Training-data leakage.** Published levels exist in model training data. Mitigations: redaction, the verbatim-quote requirement, synthetic columns with no published answer, and reading divergence rather than agreement as the primary signal.
* **Redaction removes normative rules by design.** A column's fallback or default rules were stripped with its requirements, so raters could not see them. This is intentional, since the guideline should supply the logic those rules encode, but it means some early findings measured the guideline's coverage rather than rater error.
* **Machine raters are a pre-screen.** A human inter-rater check at the boundary columns is the planned confirmation step before the rubric's bar is treated as met.
